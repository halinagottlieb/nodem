Interaction and Performativity in Digital Art Exhibitions
Date added:  2013-09-23 21:18:38
Modified:        2013-09-24 11:38:28
Author / Creator Vuokko Härmä
The interactive element of a museum or gallery visit has the potential to create a truly deep and enduring experience. The visitor is no longer seen as passive stroller but actively engaging participant whose interaction contributes to the artwork itself. Technologically mediated artworks are increasingly popular in contemporary art exhibitions. Despite being positive visiting experience interactivity can also cause feelings of self-consciousness or shyness that potentially distracts the visitor from the artwork. Digital technology is used in exhibitions to offers complex and often passively captured forms of interaction, e.g. through sensors that monitor movements or other embodied activities. This raises concerns about perceived privacy and accessibility to art as well as visitors perception of art. Sociological perspective offers a new way of seeing the interactive art exhibitions from the visitors’ perspective. Drawing on research carried out in the UK, this paper aims to contribute to the resolution of the following tension: on the one hand a positive visiting experience through interaction, and on the other feelings self-conscious and shyness induced by performative dilemma.
