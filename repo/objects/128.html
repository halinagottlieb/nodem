Technologies for supporting the inter-religious and inter-cultural dialogue at religious museums

This project presentation will describe the first results of an ongoing experimental action conducted within the frame of the European FP7 research project “MeLa- Museums in an age of migration” (www.mela-project.eu) about the topic of new museum technologies (Allen, Lupo, 2012), in the religious museum “Museo Diocesano” in Milano. The “representation” of religious issues in the public discourse of contemporary society seems to be an awkward question, especially in the attempt of museums and cultural institutions to deal with multiculturalism and intercultural dialogue: problems in contesting identities, confronting or questioning on diversities, dialoguing among identities and diversities are even amplified when the subjects of discussion are religious issues in museums. This experimental action has the aim to discuss and verify the intercultural potential of religion and its role as a privileged ‘place’ for the encounters of different religions within the Museo Diocesano involving limited groups of people of different religions (not catholic) in a guided test visit of ad hoc conceived contents. Based on a collaboration between the Design dept. of Milano Politecnico and ITIA-Institute for industrial technology and automation of CNR (National Research Council), it consists in the design of a visitor experience transforming the contemplative fruition in an interactive and contributive visit, possibly enabling intercultural dialogue too, by the use of digital technologies: i.e. the design of video narrations about some selected items of the museum property stimulating a multifaceted interpretation; the development of a digital interface and platform to enable comments, contributions with analogous issues and to facilitate the connections and relations among visitors; the intangible redesign of the space experience by requiring the visitors to perform “religiously coherent” gestures to activate the contents. The test is going to be conducted with two different types of users: experts, or “super-users”, meaning persons with a deep knowledge of their own religion (priest, theologians…) who will contribute with their religious point of view, and general public (second generation immigrants, and intercultural community resident in Milano). The reaction of visitors will be evaluated and the role of technologies in supporting forms of intercultural confrontation and understanding during the tests. The presentation will show the interactive features and the visitors participation and will discuss the way in which this experimental action could eventually inform curation within the museum, providing new information and materials for creating new parallel narrations and perspectives within the current Museo Diocesano intercultural interpretative strategies.

Sara Chiesa

Phd student in Design at Design Department of Politecnico di Milano. Master Degree in Museology at the Università Cattolica del Sacro Cuore di Milano, she works for the Extra-European Collection of the Castello Sforzesco in Milano. Her research interests are focused on design for Museums of World Cultures, ethnographic patrimony and intangibile heritage.

Rita Capurro

PhD in Design and Technologies for the enhancement of Cultural Heritage, research contract in Museology at the Design department of Politecnico di Milano. Her research interests are focused on the musealisation of religious cultural heritage.

Eleonora Lupo

Assistant professor at the Politecnico di Milano, Ph.D. in Industrial Design and Multimedia Communication at the Design Dept. of Politecnico di Milano in 2007. MA degree in 2001 in Industrial Design at the Design Faculty, Politecnico di Milano. Since 2008 Visiting Researcher and Lecturer at the School of Design Hong Kong Polytechnic University. Her research interests are focused on process and product design cultures, humanities driven innovation and design for cultural heritage. In particular on the innovative role of design as strategic and community centred approach in the enhancement and activation of tangible and intangible Cultural Heritage, processes, strategies and design techniques for the cultural heritage awareness and appropriation and sustainable development based on culture, territorial systems and intangible assets, and also for implementing sustainable local knowledge and creative and artistic activities by humanities driven innovation. Along the years, has participated to many research programs, attended several International Conferences, and has been lecturing in different schools in EU (Artesis-Antwerp, Elisava-Barcelona) Brasil (UEMG, Belo Horzonte, Unisinos-Porto Alegre), Argentina (Cordoba University) China (Tongji University Shanghai). Currently leading the following research projects: “Autentico Contemporaneo Milanese” funded by Politecnico di Milano (2011-2012), Mela-European Museum in an Age of Migration, funded by European Community (2010-2015) and involved in “Design for Cultural Heritage between History, Memory, Knowledge” funded by Italian University and Research Ministry (2010-2011). She is also founding Member of the scientific board of the Design& Humanities Lab, funded by Design Dept. and Politecnico di Milano, IT (http://designview.wordpress.com/). She teaches at the School of Design of Milano Politecnico (www.design.polimi.it).

Davide Spallazzo

Post-Doc at Politecnico di Milano, Design Department. His research interests are focused on Design research on Mobile Technology and locative media for Cultural Heritage, Game Based and Mobile Learning, Augmented and Virtual Reality for Museum Interpretation and Digitization of Cultural Heritage.

Raffaella Trocchianesi

Architect and Assistant professor at the Department of Design. She teaches at the School of Design at the Politecnico di Milano where she teaches courses and workshops in the design Degree of Interior Design. Member of the board of the PhD program in Design as well as teacher and tutor of doctoral theses. Her practice focuses on Design for Cultural Heritage in terms of layout-museum design, event design, strategy and communication for the development of the territory, the use of new technologies for cultural fruition and the relationship between design and humanities. She’s one of the founder members of the International Latin Network of Design as a Process (International Forum of Design as a Process). She worked with several national and international projcets and teaching master (including Unisinos Universidade Rio Grande do Sul, Brazil, Tongji University, Shanghai, China, University of Dundee, Scotland, National University of Cordoba , Argentina).
