Museum Without Walls
Date added:  2013-10-16 11:31:00
Modified:        2013-12-30 14:57:49
Author / Creator Karolina Ziulkoskihttp://www.karolinaziulkoski.c...
Must museums have rooms and walls? From this question came the concept of “Museum Without Walls”, a model for site-specific museums in heritage areas. The museum exists entirely in the digital realm, and is experienced on site only, providing immersive experiences with no physical interventions. More than technology, it is a concept of a museum without physical boundaries. Instead of objects, the content of the museum are stories, heard within their original context. History is told in parts using different technologies - each tells the tale most suitable to its characteristics. For this proposal the case study is São Miguel das Missões, Brazil, a former Jesuit mission, and the chosen media are video mapping and augmented reality. The daily projection serves as a collective nighttime experience that depicts the epic final battle of the mission. The AR app allows for deeper individual exploration, intimately displaying daily life in the mission in two parts: the site as it was before (a 3D model tied to GPS coordinates with an audio narration) and mini audio guides that take visitors on a journey through the site based on stories from the community. Both components have working prototypes, which can be viewed here: http://goo.gl/f7c1Q0. The app is fully functioning for iOS 6 and up, using Metaio’s SDK. The video mapping was executed in small scale on a model of the church’s façade. AR is a powerful tool that allows for a juxtaposition of digital media on a live camera view. One of its limitations is that the existing technology cannot support complex 3D models. However, there are massive investments being made in this technology, which tends to improve in little time. Video mapping is a technique that simulates 3D projection. Its limitation is also its biggest strength: the manipulation of architecture generating optical illusions.

Karolina Ziulkoski

Karolina Ziulkoski is an interactive exhibition designer from Brazil. She holds a Master of Professional Studies degree in Interactive Telecommunications from New York University, and bachelor’s degrees in Architecture from the Federal University of Rio Grande do Sul (UFRGS – Brazil – summa cum laude) and Advertising from the Pontifical University of Rio Grande do Sul (PUCRS – Brazil). Her body of work ranges from installations to applications, always focusing on delivering content through meaningful digital experiences. After spending two years in New York City, one of them interning at the American Museum of Natural History at the Exhibition Design – Interactives & Media department, she is based in southern Brazil, where she is partner at Bolota, a company focused in interactive exhibition design.
