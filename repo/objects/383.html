The Luostarinmäki Adventure – An Augmented Reality Game in an Open Air Museum

Our paper deals with the creation of an augmented reality (AR) adventure game in Luostarinmäki open air museum in Turku, Finland as a part of the Futuristic History research project. The focus of this case study was to research how to create an informative yet engaging and entertaining AR game within a museum environment. Augmented reality stands for technologies combining interactive virtual elements and information with reality in real time. The content can be presented using mobile or wearable equipment, like a smartphone, tablet or eyewear. The exact pose of the viewer must be known in order to present virtual objects on top of the camera live image in correct location. This can be achieved by taking pictures in advance and calculating a point cloud so the live image can be compared to it. This is known as keyframe-based tracking. For character creation, animations and story implementation a game-engine with external plugins can be used to make production fast and cost-efficient. The Luostarinmäki Handicrafts Museum consists of 18 blocks of original late 18th century – early 19th century buildings on their original location – the largest part of the city that survived the great fire of 1827. The museum represents crafts and town life in early 19th century. Provided with an iPad the player of the Luostarinmäki Adventure can explore the area and be able to see not only the buildings that are actually there but also the people and the life in the 1850’s as digital layers added on top of the camera view. The game mechanics are based on interaction between the player, the virtual characters and the real environment. The main challenge was the novelty and rapid advance of the technology used. Some new solutions became available and were adopted during the process. This in turn, has altered the whole process.

    Lauri Viinikkala

    M.A. Lauri Viinikkala is a PhD candidate at the University of Turku, Department of Finnish History. His doctoral thesis deals with the emergence of the agency and the world-view of the peasants in the local administration of the church parishes in south-western Finland between 1760 and 1795. He has also worked on different posts in museum sector, for example as an exhibition curator for the Museum of Cultural History of Uusikaupunki and as a researcher in the Museum Centre of Turku. He has also gained experience in questions of accessibility, visitor experience and using mixed reality in museum context.

    Olli-Pekka Leskinen

    Olli-Pekka Leskinen is team leader and curator at the Museum Centre of Turku. His areas of responsibility are pedagogical operations and exhibitions at Luostarinmäki Handicrafts Museum and the Pharmacy Museum and Qwensel House. His previous experience includes working as curator in planning and implementing permanent exhibitions for Turku Castle and Lohja Museum. He also has experience in cultural history collections and the development of museum operations. In addition to working in the museum sector, he has worked as a qualified teacher of history and social studies in secondary schools and high schools. In the museum sector he is specialised in museum pedagogy and exhibitions, as well as information and communication technology.

    Olli I. Heimo

    M.Sc. Olli I. Heimo is an information system scientist and doctoral student currenly working as a researcher in the University of Turku. His main research fields of interest are information ethics, governmental information systems, usability and videogames.

    Juha-Pekka Arimaa

    M.Sc. (Tech.) Juha-Pekka Arimaa is a project researcher at the University of Turku, Business and Innovation Development (BID) Unit. His educational background considers software engineering, business economics and hypermedia. He has several years’ experience in software development and project work and currently he acts as responsible of technical matters in a research project. Arimaa has experience in both back and front-end development with multiple different programming languages.

    Timo Korkalainen

    Timo Korkalainen does research and software development on mixed reality technologies at the Technology Research Center at the University of Turku. He is experienced in creating both content and technology for use in digital media environment. His work ranges from graphical design and live video productions to developing concepts, services and applications for the web and mobile devices. He is an advocate of agile software production methods and is one of the core organizers of the annual Turku Agile Day conference. His bachelor thesis he wrote about the usability of web content management systems and is currently finalizing his Master studies at the University of Turku.

    Tuomas Mäkilä

    D.Sc.(Tech.), Tuomas Mäkilä is a senior research fellow at the University of Turku, where he has researched and taught software engineering since 2004. His research interest is on the software development methods and tools. Mäkilä is also a game enthusiast who has co-founded one of the first professional game studios in Turku.

    Seppo Helle

    Lic. Tech. Seppo Helle works currently as a system specialist at the University of Turku, Technology Research Center. He has done most of his career in Nokia Mobile Phones and Nokia Oy, working on user interface and user experience related jobs. He participated in mobile phone UI platform development, producing application designs as well as style guides for Nokia mobile phone platforms. Helle has participated in numerous product development projects and has led user interface target setting for product programs. He has authored or co-authored more than 10 patented inventions on user interface area while at Nokia. Helle received his Master of Sciences, Electrical Engineering, diploma from Helsinki University of Technology in 1987 and Licentiate of Technology from the same university in 1992.

    Teijo Lehtonen

    D.Sc. (Tech.) Teijo Lehtonen works as a Senior Research Fellow at the University of Turku, Technology Research Center, where he leads the Embedded and Mixed-Reality Systems research group. Lehtonen has D.Sc. (Tech.) degree in electronics from University of Turku in 2009, and he was granted the Academy of Finland post-doctoral researcher’s grant for 2011-2013. D.Sc. (Tech.) Lehtonen has been the principal investigator (PI) in numerous research and development projects with the total value of several million euros. Lehtonen has published over 30 scientific articles.
