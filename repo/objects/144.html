Rooftops at Dawn – A Novel as Locative Walk
Date added:  2013-10-15 21:25:57
Modified:        2013-10-15 21:25:58
Author / Creator Zsófia Ruttkayhttp://techlab.mome.hu/ruttkay
The multimedia, GPS-based mobile app enhances the 2012 exhibition of the Petőfi Literary Museum beyond its walls, celebrating the 100th anniversary of the Hungarian writer Géza Ottlik in 2012. Parts from his novel “Rooftops at Dawn”, taking place in the1930s in Budapest, may be read and/or listened to during a GPS-based walk in the city. This experience is enriched by browsing archive photographs, news videos, movie-extracts and articles, offered in thematic galleries. The collections highlight topics and lifestyles occurring in the pieces of text. Thus the “reader” is placed in an era-evoking environment of a mixture of the virtual recalling the past, and the reality of today. The application was based on a concept born at the multidisciplinary course “Digital Museum” of the Creative Technology Lab at MOME. The final product is a joint success of a multi-institutional collaboration: over 300 archive documents were made available from 13 collections, technology was taken care of by experts from the academic institution MTA SZATKI, and further support was given by local government and small businesses. By offering classic content - such as literary text and old documents - as an interconnected mix of experience, the application has the potential to reach a wider audience for culture, including youngsters. The edutainment experience has the ultimate goal of webbing the appetite of the youth for reading the entire book, and literature, in general. It also presents a new way of location-based multimedia storytelling of literature. Links: About the walk: http://techlab.mome.hu/rooftops_at_dawn/ MOME Creative Technology Lab: http://techlab.mome.hu/

    Zsófia Ruttkay

    Zsófia Ruttkay is associate professor at the Moholy-Nagy University of Art and Design in Budapest. She graduated in Mathematics at the Eötvös Loránd University in Budapest, got her PhD in Computer Graphics, and – before joining MOME in 2009 - had had an international academic carrier of 25 years in computer science, particularly, artificial intelligence, computer graphics and human-computer interaction. She has a publication record with over 140 items. She has been participating in several international EU projects. She is the founder and head of the Creative Technology Lab at the Média Institute. She is involved in teaching programming and communication technology for art students, and running projects on applying the technology for socially relevant, useful and appealing applications. Currenty with her lab they focus on two topics: interactive books and the usage of digital technologies for cultural heritage applications, in the broadest sense. The projects are realized by interdisciplinary teams, with students, national and international partners. Some projects carried out in the Lab have gained broad recognition and won national and international prizes. Zsófia Ruttkay advocates that entirely novel ideas and applications can emerge on the intersection of art, science and technology, which are playful, witty and visually appealing (often the technology is packed in an in itself beautiful enveloping furniture or is completely hidden). She also believes in the huge potentials of such hybrid new genres in educating and connecting people of today and tomorrow.
