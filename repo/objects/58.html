Evolving Architectural History and Digital Intangible Heritage
Date added:  2013-09-23 14:43:39
Modified:        2013-09-23 14:43:39
Author / Creator Tessa Morrison
Author / Creator Helen Giggins
Author / Creator Nicholas Foulcher
In a project currently underway in Newcastle, Australia, a mobile application has been developed to reconstruct Victorian Newcastle. The focus on Victorian Newcastle was due to this being a pivotal time in the young city’s development. This type of project is becoming increasing popular, with applications for both heritage and tourism purposes. The mobile application has been developed by the authors and in November it will be made available to the public with an exhibition at the Newcastle Regional Museum. However, the long-term plan of the application is to have public purposes, displaying heritage and tourist information, and academic purposes, holding embedded data to enhance urban studies. A digital architectural reconstruction through different eras of each site can be viewed in three levels; 1) the lost architecture 2) structural alterations to existing buildings and 3) changes of the details of the building i.e. window, doors, decoration etc. Depending on the information available for each site, such as drawings, photographs and plans, it is possible to track the architectural development of each site . This is of course possible without digital technology, by simply viewing the information physically. What makes a mobile application desirable is that is makes the data easy to handle and, importantly, it is more accessible to both academics and the public. Community engagement is a key element to the success of such a project as it provides much of the data, particularly photographs. In addition, the mobile application makes it possible to view many sites chronologically with relative ease making it possible to analyse in detail architectural changes across the city, changing purposes and structure of the building, and trends in renovations and alterations. This paper outlines the advantages and projected outcomes to Architectural History.

    Tessa Morrison

    Researcher, School of Architecture and Built Environment, Newcastle, Australia. Tessa Morrison was awarded her PhD in 2004, and in 2007 joined the School of Architecture and Built Environment. In 2007 as a Research Fellow in Architectural History and in 2008 she was awarded Australian Research Council Post-Doctoral Fellowship, Discovery Grant, the project was entitled ‘Isaac Newton s Temple of Solomon and his analysis of sacred architecture: An interpretation and discussion of Babson Manuscript 0434.’ From this research she has focused on seventeenth and eighteenth century sacred architecture, along with digitial reconstructions of this architecture, using Archicad and 3D Studio Max. Her curent research is considering how the architectural historian can use mobile technologies to enhance historic and design research. This has lead to a project entitled Timeline Newcastle which is in collaboration with Dr Helen Giggins and Nicholas Foulcher. This project aims to reconstruct the architectural history of Newcastle over its 200 year European history. The project reconstructing Victorian Newcastle is the first stage of Timeline Newcastle.

    Helen Giggins

    Researcher, School of Architecture and Built Environment, Newcastle, Australia. Helen Giggins was awarded her PhD in Computer Science from the University of Newcastle, Australia in 2009. Her current research interests are diverse, ranging across disaster management and recovery, use of ITC in education and architectural history. Her previous research interests include privacy preserving data publishing, privacy preserving data mining, trust management and Internet communications.

    Nicholas Foulcher

    Phd Candidate of Architecture, School of Architecture and Built Environment, Newcastle, Australia. Nicholas Foulcher is a Casual Academic and Research Assistant at the School of Architecture and Built Environment at the University of Newcastle, Australia. He holds a Bachelor of Design (Arch), a Master of Architecture (Hons1) and is currently a PhD candidate (Arch) at the University of Newcastle. His current projects include studies into technologies impact on architectural education, and the reconstruction and preservation of architectural heritage using Augmented Reality.
