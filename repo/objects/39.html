Introspection by Interaction: Visualizing the Visitor Experience in the Digital Container
Date added:  2013-09-17 21:56:25
Modified:        2013-09-19 16:55:19
Author / Creator Anita Kocsis
The focus of this presentation is on the contribution of visitors felt in response to the theorization and design of the semi-immersive interactive exhibition environment known as the digital container. The analysis of a participant’s emotion and co-experience in the digital container aims to contribute to an emerging syntax of digital space and interaction design that predominantly focuses on the technologies of form and content and undertheorizes the pivotal point of the container; namely, the experience of the visitor. As feelings and experiences are abstract and inaccessible to objective scrutiny, it is difficult to describe the exactness of the experience. Equally, measurements of such responses are difficult. Aitkin notes the limitation of the use of language for the measurement of feelings. Despite this difficulty, Mark Johnson’s proposition that we understand the world because we have a body argues that metaphor is key to our understanding of perception, imagination and thought. Johnson’s ‘image-schemes’; abstract representations of process, thinking and visualisation inform the design semiotics and interaction of an experimental artifact used to ascertain a visitor’s experience of the digital container profiled in this presentation. The prototype artifact, titled the I Sho U, invites introspection through visitor engagement. The artifact is mediated by interaction and aesthetics. This type of visual analysis is atypical of traditional visitor studies or cognitive measurement, provides a pulse for stakeholders interested in visitor response and is an alternative in the quest for quantification of qualitative abstract states of emotion in a digital space.

    Anita Kocsis

    Head of Design, Society and Culture in the Faculty of Design, Swinburne University of Technology, Melbourne, Australia. Dr. Anita Kocsis is Head of Design, Society and Culture in the Faculty of Design, Swinburne University of Technology in Melbourne, Australia. Anita’s creative, commercial and academic achievement spans a design and technology nexus evidenced by intermedia exhibitions in public and private institutions. Anita’s PhD at the i-Cinema Centre for Interactive Cinema Research, University of New South Wales, titled ‘Co-Designing New Media Spaces’, positioned the phenomenon of the visitor experience as a design strategy for mediating between repertoires of practice in interdisciplinary new media exhibitions. Anita’s articulation across research and teaching focuses on how design can facilitate the multilateral process of meaning construction that unfolds between content providers, designers and audiences. Her work in the neuroaffective research cluster of the faculty focuses on the phenomenon of visitor feelings in interactive and immersive experiences for museums, galleries and the digital humanities. She is experimenting with interactive design systems for visualizing the pulse of the visitor experience which can be used by stakeholders of human-centred virtual system design and emergent stereoscopic and panoramic immersive architectures.
