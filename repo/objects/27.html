Hong Kong Maritime Museum
Date added:  2013-09-17 20:14:11
Modified:        2013-09-23 13:05:36
Author / Creator Richard Wesley
The Hong Kong Maritime Museum operates as a public charity in Hong Kong. Within a city that is filled by government operated museums, the institution is in the unusual position of being traditionally required to raise its operating costs from private funds and commercial activities. In the past, the Hong Kong shipping industry has provided the largest amount of this financial support. More recently, the Hong Kong Government has generously agreed to cover the estimated HK $115,000,000 capital cost of relocating the institution from Stanley to Central and to contribute towards the museum’s annual operating expenses. As the museum moves to a newly renovated space at Central Pier 8, and plans unfold for 15 new galleries filled with unique artefacts from Hong Kong’s maritime past, the institution faces challenges to expand its stakeholders and make new partnerships in the community. By effectively exploiting new technology and intangible heritage, opportunities have been identified to help the museum grow and reach new audiences. The museum believes the introduction of new media and digital learning opportunities to the public will help better serve the learning communities of Hong Kong and set the museum apart from other similar cultural organizations in Hong Kong. With this in mind, HKMM chose City University to be the vector for helping visitors understand ‘the known, the unknown and the unknown unknowns’ of its world class exhibit, the Qing Dynasty Pirates Scroll. The storyline of this 18-metre scroll revolves around the early nineteenth century anti-piracy campaign of the Jiaqing Emperor who commissioned the Governor General of Liangguang (Guangdong and Guanxi) Bailing to defeat the Zheng pirate federation who operated in the Pearl River Delta and beyond. Museum Director Richard Wesley will argue that only by the adoption of sophisticated digital presentation techniques can modern cultural institutions hope to encourage their audiences to fully engage with the subtle and complex cultural knowledge locked within key icons such as the Pirates Scroll.

    Richard Wesley

    Director. Hong Kong Maritime Museum. Richard Wesley has served as the Director of the Hong Kong Maritime Museum since December of 2010. He was brought on specifically to spearhead the museum’s move from Stanley to Central Pier 8. Richard has extensive experience in the management and operation of museum facilities across Australia. He has worked on projects in New South Wales, Tasmania, Norfolk Island and Western Australia, along with lecturing in Museum Management at University of Sydney for five years. Richard’s previous industry positions include: Director of the Norfolk Island Museum, Manager of the Port Arthur Historic Site and Director of Museums and Properties for the National Trust of Australia (NSW). He holds a Master of Arts Degree from the University of Tasmania and a Diploma of Museum Studies (Sydney) and is the author of a wide range of museum reports completed over the last fifteen years. He has a particular interest in house museums, marine archaeology and the use of objects in storytelling.
