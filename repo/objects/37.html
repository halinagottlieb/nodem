Poetic Interpretation of Asian Art: Using New Media for Storytelling and Experimentation
Date added:  2013-09-17 21:31:26
Modified:        2013-09-19 13:09:16
Author / Creator Herminia Din
Love it or hate it, new media presentations have become an important element of the museum experience or experiment. Museum professionals realize that in order to reach contemporary audiences they must communicate in ways that they understand. To remain relevant to contemporary society, museums must attract and maintain an active and interested audience — an audience who is primarily accustomed to being entertained and educated through the latest technology. In light of this, many museums in Asia today have incorporated new media interpretation into their programs and exhibits — and they continue to do so, despite the growing popularity, and proliferation, of social networking Web sites. This presentation will showcase several recent new media installations in Asian museums using immersive and interactive new media for storytelling and experimentation. Through multiple large screen projections, on-site specific theatrical and motion detective installations, these new media displays were produced to interpret, enhance and promote exhibit themes and goals. These installations are no longer a digital reproduction of the artwork but use new media as a tool to create an exquisite environment so visitors can ‘walk into’ a century old Chinese scroll, hearing a fisherman’s echo through a cloud covered mountain valley, catching a summer breeze from a lotus pond, or having a 3D animated butterfly or a cricket land on your palm. Using new media as a storytelling tool adds a poetic touch through multiple sensory modalities. This has revolutionized the traditional academic approach of understanding Asian art. This session hopes not only to showcase an array of the best practices in Asia, but also to discuss current trends of using new media for interpretation and new perspectives of using technology in the context of museum education.

Herminia Din

Dr. Din is an associate professor of art education at the University of Alaska Anchorage. She was the Web producer at the Children’s Museum of Indianapolis and education technologist at the Indianapolis Museum of Art. In 2005, she partnered with the University of Alaska Museum of the North in Fairbanks on the LearnAlaska project, and facilitated a docent-training program using Internet2 videoconferencing for a distance-delivered program. She presented at SIGGRAPH Educator’s Program on educational gaming for museums, and delivered a speech on using animation and interactive virtual technology to enhance museum learning at SIGGRAPH ASIA 2008. She collaborated with colleagues at the Metropolitan Museum of Art, American Museum of Natural History, California Academy of Sciences, and de Young Fine Art Museum to offer teacher development programs focusing on art and science integration. In 2007, she co-edited The Digital Museum: A Think Guide that offers an in-depth investigation into how and why museums are experimenting with new technology, and co-authored Unbound by Place or Time: Museums and Online Learning (2009) and All Together Now: Museums and Online Collaborative Learning (2011). Her research focuses on object-based learning and evaluation of the effectiveness of museum online resources. She addresses the transformation of teaching and learning by using new technologies, and aspects of emerging technology for implementing creative initiatives to enhance museum education. She holds a doctorate in art education from Ohio State University and presents regularly on museum and technology at national and international conferences.
