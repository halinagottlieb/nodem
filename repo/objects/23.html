Conception of and Recent Progress on the Digital Mogao Grottoes / ‘数字莫高窟’的构想及其新进展
Date added:  2013-09-17 16:20:23
Modified:        2014-04-23 18:03:35
Author / Creator Xudong Wang
Founded in the 4th Century, the Dunhuang Mogao Grottoes are one of the brightest pearls along the ancient Silk Road, containing what is arguably the largest and richest collections of Buddhist art in existence. The Dunhuang Academy is continuously exploring new methods for conserving and sustaining the priceless heritage of human culture. As part of this mission, the Academy is developing the Digital Mogao Grottoes project, which is aimed at the preservation and sustainable use of the Mogao grottoes through the utilization of cutting-edge digital technologies. The Digital Mogao Grottoes project includes the acquisition of data from and processing of Mogao frescoes and painted sculptures, as well as the development of a digital database. This project uses a variety of digital technologies to present the art in the Mogao grottoes from various perspectives. A Visitor Centre will be established at the Mogao site along with a worldwide touring exhibition of holdings from the collection. A vast array of digital products and technologies will be utilized for the construction of the database and the traveling exhibition. The development of the Digital Mogao Grottoes project is the most effective way to protect the grottoes, as well as to promote general research about the Mogao site and its numerous innovations. 始建于公元四世纪的敦煌莫高窟可称之为古代丝绸之路上最为璀璨的明珠,是现存规模最 大、营造时间最长、内容最丰富的佛教艺术殿堂。 敦煌研究院不断探索长久保存和永续利用珍贵人类文化遗产的方法。随着数字技术的发 展,我们提出了建设’数字莫高窟’构想。这是利用数字科技长久保存和有效利用莫高窟艺术 的系统工程。 ‘数字莫高窟’包括石窟壁画、彩塑及其相关信息的采集、处理与数字资源库建设;采用多种 数字媒体技术多角度多视野展示莫高窟石窟艺术,其中包括利用新建的游客中心、敦煌研 究院门户网站以及在世界各地举办敦煌艺术展的机会,让观众领略敦煌莫高窟艺术的魅力; 基于数字资源库开发各类数字化产品等。 ‘数字莫高窟’的建设,是实现敦煌莫高窟保护与利用双赢的最有效手段,也将推动敦煌莫高 窟保护、研究、弘扬工作模式的创新。

    Xudong Wang

    Deputy Director, Dunhuang Academy, Gansu Province, China. Wang Xudong graduated from the Geology Department of Lanzhou University in 1990, receiving his Master’s Degree in Environmental Science and PhD in Engineering from Lanzhou University in 1998 and 2002. He is currently Researcher and Standing Vice-Director of the Dunhuang Academy, and Standing Vice-Director of the National Research Center for the Conservation of Ancient Wall Paintings. He is also a guest professor at Lanzhou University and Northwest University of China. He was a Visiting Fellow at Tokyo National Research Institute for Cultural Properties in 1993–1994 and 2000–2001, and a Visiting Fellow at the Getty Conservation Institute in 2005 and 2010. Professor Wang has successively presided over the preservation of countless frescoes and ruins across Dunhuang, Tibet, Xinjiang and Ningxia, and has chaired over 20 research projects for the Ministry of Science and Technology, the State Administration of Cultural Heritage and Gansu Province. He was named to the list of Excellent Science and Technological Workers, and has been awarded the rank of Outstanding Expert from both The Ministry of Culture and Gansu province. He also has been awarded seven National Invention Awards and provincial scientific and technological progress awards. Professor Wang is also president of the ISRM Commission on Preservation of Ancient Sites (CPAS), Vice Chairman of the China Association for the Preservation Technology of Cultural Relics, Vice Chairman and Secretary General, Commission on Preservation and Consolidation Engineering of Ancient Sites (CSRME), Committee member for the International Council on Monuments and Sites (ICOMOS), Vice Chairman of the China Dunhuang Grottoes Conservation Research Foundation and Council Member of the United States Dunhuang Foundation.
