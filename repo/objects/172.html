The cross-cultural communication features of a distributed museum mobile guide application platform: a concept design
Date added:  2013-10-16 12:31:27
Modified:        2013-10-16 12:31:27
Author / Creator Shuchen Wang
The ubiquitous mobile and web technology has brought Malraux’s museum idea to the global world as well as our pockets, literally. It has become common now to regard smartphones as a medium for museum outreach and exhibition interpretation. However, the cross-cultural aspect of this usage is not much discussed. As museums do face international audiences, it is important to look into methods of cross-cultural communication not only on site but also online in the mobile and web environment. This article at first investigates the conventional cross-cultural communication practices on site of the museum exhibition, then undertakes a comparison studies of a few museum mobile guide apps (e.g. of Guggenheim, Louvre, and MoMA), and at last draws a blueprint of cross-cultural communication concept design for a distributed museum mobile guide app platform. That is currently under development in Finland for the Chinese museums in terms of art exhibition. Both on site and online, language is the first target to convert if to approach the foreign audience. It seems to be the only obstacle the mobile apps of those world-leading museums have resolved by far. The concept design will take into account the differences in mobile preference, mobile market situation, ways of employment, characteristic of operator, social media trend, and museum visitor behavior, etc. As the mobile virtual space is more flexible than the exhibition gallery hall, it certainly shall carry out the museum’s cross-cultural communication with better efficiency and greater significance.

    Shuchen Wang

    Dept of New Media, School of Arts, Deisgn and Architecture, Aalto University, Helsinki, Finland
