The other way round: from semantic search to collaborative curation
Date added:  2013-10-14 23:29:54
Modified:        2014-01-15 18:53:48
Author / Creator Chantal Eschenfelderhttp://www.staedelmuseum.de
Author / Creator Karsten Greschhttp://www.softwareag.com
Founded in 1816, the Städel museum is a well renowned German art museum and faces several challenges: We’ve 100.000+ exhibits. But we can only show 99% of them. We’re engaged in more than 50 different educational programs. But we haven’t found a way addressing the diversified global audience needs through digital channels. We jumped into the digital world starting to digitise our content centrally, making it findable through semantic search. In a joint project we developed an exhibit platform realising a concept we call “digital strolling”. Digital strolling redefines digital visits of a museum. Physically strolling through a museum means interacting with others, getting inspirations and exploring new content. The Google Museum approach is not a generically digital way as people would get information how an exhibit looks like and where it resides. Transferring this information cannot replace impressions given facing the original. Real digital offerings should enrich the visitor’s initial interest, deliver more than requested and ignite interest in new topics. The semantic search engine achieves this by intuitively matching digitised multimedia content which is based on a complex system indexing unstructured and structured data related to our exhibits. Compared to lexical searching, the visitor gets a nudge to new but relevant content without being aware of it before. It supports digital visitors finding a path through vast offerings of digitised exhibits: Semantic searching brings up results being intrinsically relevant. Digital visitors can save their visiting paths and also share and discuss with them with others. This goes toward high-quality consumer curation thanks to the inner semantic logic. Though not being comparable to art historians’ curations, it helps digital visitors expressing themselves better than submitting Facebook “likes” or pictures on Pinterest and alike. (Live demo planned). Project funded by the LOEWE initiative/Germany/Hesse (HA 321/12-11). Cooperating partners: University+State Library Darmstadt, Software AG, media transfer AG, nterra GmbH, House of IT.

Chantal Eschenfelder

Chantal Eschenfelder is Head of Education of the Städel Museum and Liebighaus Skulpturensammlung and the Schirn Kunsthalle in Frankfurt/Main, Germany. Born 1965, she studied History of Art, German Literature and American Civilization in Paris and Munich where she earned her Ph.D. She has published books on French and Italian art in times of manierism as well as educational books for children.

Karsten Gresch

Karsten Gresch (1968) is a lawyer and computer engineer. He earned his assessor iuris (law master equivalent) at the Freie Universität Berlin, worked as an advocate and finally switched to the IT business where he worked for different companies (amongst them Skandia) in various technical positions. At his current employer, Software AG, he is the technical responsible for the cloud media platform, a research project offering a semantic search engine for art exhibits, books and more (http://is.gd/cloudmediasag). Karsten also founded the Lean Museum Startup initiative (http://leanmusemstartup.com) and has special interests in entrepreneurship, business model generation and all facets of innovation and interdisciplinarity. Thanks to becoming well-bred through family and education, he loves the arts, e.g. Venetian painting from Renaissance to Tiepolo, contemporary arts, poetry, classical music (both historic and contemporary) and the many interfaces between arts and IT. Karsten is married and father of five (2+3) children.
