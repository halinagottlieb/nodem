Museum/site: historie-online Country: Denmark Developers: Dansk Historisk Fællesråd, woth a number of memberorganisations, including these Dansk Kulturhistorisk Museumsforening Sponsor partnerships: Submitted by: Dansk Kulturhistorisk Museumsforening - Dansk Historisk Fællesråd Project website:http://www.historie-online.dk Project status: completed Project type: Web based/Virtual Application Start year: 2003 End year: 2003 Summary: Since January 2003 he first Danish e-zine on history has been a reality at least for 2003, which is a trial-period. The idea has been to create at historysite dealing with \\\"history\\\" as a topic and with as many historical institutions as possible. Most people with an interest in history are interested in \\\"history\\\" as such, and not necessarily in a particular institution, allthough this may also often happen. But up to now, there has not been any website trying to satisfy popular historical interest as such. Where would one find a calendar with historical arrangements and exhibitions only? Academic seminars on archaeology or preservation of photos? New books or the story behind Easter? Excavations, finds, riddles from 19th. century rural Denmark - or a PC-desktop with winter scenes from museums? And all in one place? This is among the things you will find on www.historie-online.dk, “history-online” The website is organized and maintained by Dansk Historisk Fællesråd (DHF) Danish Historical Common Association which is an umbrella organization for all historical institutions, university departments, museums, archives, historical societies, local historical associations etc. in Denmark interested in history. Especially the museums have prooved to be most interested in keeping the site “on-line” with news. The aim has been to create a website containing news and resources with relation to “everything historical” in Denmark. The site is organized in different sections, the news-section being the largest. Among the news are articles with information about new special exhibitions, new archaeological finds etc. i.e. everything, that has a natural place in a historical weekly. Most articles have screen length and are illustrated, and of course the pages promote the relevant institutions by linking to them. The general idea is to create a focuspoint or window to the rest of the world, where primarily the member institutions such as museums, can present their activities, books etc. But articles and discussions about issues of relevance to people with historical interest are also part of the site. Among the recent major topics have been: documenting immigrant-history at the private archives, if “obsolete” objects in museums should stay in storage for ever and whether or not a statue of the naval hero Niels Juel should be moved or not. In connection with the presentation of these viewpoints, polls have been set up, so that the readers may give their opinion too. Each week www.historie-online.dk presents a list of newly published history-books (including novels etc) as well as book-reviews (”BogFeature”). Two students give tips on TV-programmes on history and archaeology, and events like Easter and Mother’s Day of course get a historical angle. In time, historie-online also hopes to be able to bring more news from the academic world, e.g. when a thesis is published, a scholarship given or a new research-project started. As indicated www.historie-online.dk hope to be of interest for everyone working with Danish history. And since history is not just serious business but also great fun, the site has a section with exactly this title: History for Fun. This is the place to find PC-motifs, crack the old riddles or examine ones skills as an archaeologist by “mending” an iron-age jar from bits and pieces. At the present www.historie-online.dk has an average of about 200 visitors each day, and about 3-4 new comers sign up for the newsletter each day. Due to economic reasons, there has been almost no advertising, so in this context, the group behind historie-online find the result so far pleasing. At the moment most of the work is done by voluntary labour, by members of the board in Dansk Historisk Fællesråd, interested students or historians. This is of course a difficult situation, since editing and producing an e-zine of this kind is a large task, and the hope is, of course, that the DHF and partners will be able to fund the activity, thus making it more stabile.
