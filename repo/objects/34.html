Imaging Data: Computation, Analytics, Reverse Predictive Practices
Date added:  2013-09-17 21:14:19
Modified:        2013-09-23 20:41:57
Author / Creator Lewis Lancaster
The successful creation of large amounts of digital data has placed scholars in all fields, and the humanities, at a point where management issues have become crucial. It is no longer sufficient to provide search and retrieval capabilities that return thousands of examples. In order to achieve the best possible use of information, we must seek for the most efficient methods of communicating the presence and meanings of a wide range of analytic patterns. One necessary component of this presentation of explanatory analysis is image. The Electronic Cultural Atlas Initiative (ECAI) at the University of California, Berkeley has been exploring methods of accomplishing these strategies through the use of Geographic Information Systems (GIS) mapping of cultural features. As part of the context for the cultural maps, an added feature is the use of computation and analysis for large textual corpora such as canonic texts, archival documents and digital records of artifacts. Pattern searching is accomplished by changing natural language text into abstract images (blue dots) that display word occurrence and patterning by color changes. In addition, the software being developed provides the user with a number of different graphs, charts, time lines and comparative matches. A recent addition to the services offered by the Open Platform is predictive analysis, normally used to indicate future expectations, but now reversed so as to identify anomalous patterning found in past data sources. A new focus will be on how to collect annotations from scholars who create these data and analytics and with these statements bring into being a new type of publication that is tied to the digital, rather than codex format, or a copy of previously established methods of dissemination.
