Generate, Interact, Connect - The future of digital guiding
Date added:  2013-10-16 09:03:24
Modified:        2013-10-16 09:05:41
Author / Creator André Seirafihttp://www.fluxguide.com
Author / Creator Tomáš Mikeskahttp://www.fluxguide.com
Technology-based guiding in museums still lags behind its potential. This is not so much because there is no effort towards new technologies. In contrary, plenty of iPads and similar devices populate exhibition spaces around the world. But still, existing digital guides are mostly seen as some kind of isolated information storages. The potential of interconnecting guides with each other or with the exhibition space is hardly ever exploited. This paper shortly outlines two museum use cases, of next digital guiding solutions: 1. Social Interaction between visitors: “My Favourite Object” at Nasher Museum, North Carolina, USA 2. Interaction with the exhibition space: “Sun Musical” at the Virtuality Centre, Riyadh, Saudi Arabia Wireless technology and open interfaces allow exciting interconnections between digital guides and exhibits, as well as between digital guides and other technology at the exhibition space. At a large museum about the history of communication in Saudi Arabia, digital visitor tablets will connect with the exhibit “Sun Musical”. The exhibit itself is about to demonstrate the variety of medial representations of the world, in this case the solar planetary system. The mechanical installation simulates the solar planets rotating around the sun. Besides this visual / object representation, planets also generate a musical sound every time they pass a specific line during their rotation. To amplify the multiplicity of possible representations, visitors will be able to get a synchronized depiction of the real exhibit on their digital tablet guide. Via a wireless interface to the exhibit, planets and their positions are synchronized in real time with the exhibits planets. Additionally, also the visitor tablets generate musical sound each time planets cross the line (see image). The audio output will be played on the tablets themselves, i.e. it can be heard by others at the exhibition space. Moreover, different instruments can be selected. The potential result is a collaborative musical orchestra generated by visitors and the exhibit.
