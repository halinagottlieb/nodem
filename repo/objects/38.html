Collaboration, Innovation and the Future of Museums
Date added:  2013-09-17 21:41:26
Modified:        2013-09-19 12:44:25
Author / Creator Timothy Hart
Museum Victoria is Australia’s largest public museum organisation, incorporating Melbourne Museum, Scienceworks, the Immigration Museum and the World Heritage- listed Royal Exhibition Building. In 2009–10, 2.1 million people visited the museums and the website attracted more than 8.6 million views. Museum Victoria is responsible for the State collection of more than 16 million items, which form an invaluable record of Victoria’s environmental and cultural history. Researchers, many of whom are world experts in their field, work in the areas of natural sciences, history and technology, Indigenous cultures and museology. This research adds to the communities’ understanding of critical contemporary issues such as reconciliation, biodiversity, climate change and cultural diversity. The world’s largest and oldest museums have all been operating for over 150 years and it is important to see museums in the context of the thinking and acting with long-term aims and ambitions. Over the past 150 years there has been massive technological change (steam to nuclear, walking to flight, flags to mobiles, etc.) none at a faster pace than the changes that the digital age has introduced. The effects of this change cover, as they do in our everyday lives, all areas of museum operations. Running the ‘core’ technologies that allow a ‘modern’ museum to operate effectively are a big challenge — there are never the resources you need to maintain pace with the ever increasing demands of change, but it’s always better to be leading the change. Museum Victoria will be used to outline how large multi-campus museums are run — and what separate departments and divisions are needed to effectively manage day to day operations, develop new exhibitions, manage collections, operate cafes and shops, car parks and run the buildings, etc. It’s a complex business with a lot going on behind the scenes. This presentation will focus on technology — how we do what we do with examples of our work in exhibitions and online and, the systems that underlie the multimedia we use in our exhibitions. The paper will briefly discuss our management models and explain what we do differently — what works and what doesn’t.

    Timothy Hart

    Tim Hart is Director, Information, Multimedia and Technology, Museum Victoria. Tim is responsible for creating a visitor experience that is engaging, interesting and continues to expand our audience through digital technology and the provision of reliable, world-class business information management systems. Also, responsible for the implementation and management of Information Technology systems throughout Museum Victoria.
