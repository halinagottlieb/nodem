Crowdsourcing by social media – Harvesting the 80’s
Date added:  2013-10-16 10:32:06
Modified:        2013-10-16 10:32:14
Author / Creator Greet Vanderhaegenhttp://www.huisvanalijn.be
The House of Alijn is a museum about the culture of everyday life located in Ghent, Belgium. In order to deploy and pass on meaning- engerdering ideas, stories and images with both young and old, the museum tries to share her passion for the heritage of everyday culture in a dynamic, inspiring and mind-broadening way. The public is assigned various roles and the role of expert is shared. In the latest years crowdsourcing is initiated in pilots, from social tagging to initiatives to enlarge the collection in co-creation with the public. The choice for an experimental role as creative and social hub keeps alive the connection between public and the museum. A few months ago the museum set up a trilling online pilot, titled ‘time for the 80’s’(www.tijdvoor80.be). The objective is driven by two main questions: How can we as museum capture this era ‘online’ and how to bring it ‘onsite’ in the museum presentation, regarding the way the 80’s are now ‘(re)collected, (re)interpreted and (re)used’ by the public in memories, stories, objects, music, images at the different online platforms and social media. This project is set up as a pilot about the way the museum can use social media for communication, collecting and talking about content in an other than the traditional way. Together with the public we want to search via social media channels for the sound, the colour en the face(s) of the 80’s. The public is invited to connect and to share, (hash)tag, pin, tweet, upload, blog, post, like, comment, mention… images, stories and ideas, memories, emotions and inspirations about the 80’s of themselves and others via #tijdvoor80 and to built up an 80′s dashboard. Via uploads on the social media channels of their choice the public can share information and knowledge about this decade.

    Greet Vanderhaegen

    Greet Vanderhaegen (°1969) works since 2000 as collection manager in the House of Alijn. She studied Art History at the University of Ghent and Library & Information Sciences at the University of Antwerp.
