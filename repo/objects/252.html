Timetravels in the Industrial landscape of Norrkoping
Date added:  2013-12-31 13:24:55
Modified:        2013-12-31 13:25:04
Museum/site: The City museum of Norrkoping and Norrkoping Visualization and Interaction Studio, NVIS Country: Sweden Developers: Martin Etherton Friberg, Kosta Economou, Staffan Klashed, Martin Rasmusson, Ulrica Stjernqvist, Linus Olsson and Jonas Hjalmarsson. Sponsor partnerships: Submitted by: Norrkoping Visualization and Interaction Studio, NVIS Project website http://www.nvis.itn.liu.se and www.fargadvarld.com Project status: completed Project type: Large scale/Immersive Installation Start year: 2001 End year: 2004 Summary: VR technology is commonly presented on a screen, in a VR cube or projected on large surfaces before a seated audience. Similarities to other media are obvious, for example TV, computer games and theatres. Because VR is produced in real time, we suffer from poor resolution and richness in detail of fully articulated materials. The presentation suffers in comparison to pre-rendered material. Premises based on such criterion disadvantage VR technique, especially concerning contexts with which we are familiar. It therefore becomes necessary to create a situation - a \\\"now\\\" - in which the audience participates in the presentation as it happens. In our performance, nothing is prepared. The whole presentation coalesces to a single homogeneous entity, including the audience, the computer graphics and the actual environment in which the manifestation is held, which enables us to travel in space and time. The graphics engine through which the traditional VR representation is pipelined must be considered only a tool for selling the story, not the story in itself. The engine must be smooth enough to avoid interruptions to the suspension of disbelief provided by content. It is more important to provide linear level of detail, hence smoothness, than to provide a higher level of detail that can not be maintained throughout the VR world or at constant framerate. Intentions: This is a attempt to make VR available for a larger audience than VR usually is: for exemple museum visitors and students. But it will of course also be useful for researchers as well. Tech spec Space/Time must constitute an essential part of the story, if we are to succeed in truly demonstrating the advantages of using VR technique. In our Industrial landscape project, we have built a temporal database spanning seventy years of architecture in the area of interest. Parts of the database are extracted and rendered according to our position in the four dimensions, providing a seamless real-time world spanning not only space but also time. Exercises in Drama are the principal methods for achieving the situation, - the “now” -. Drama means Action. When engaged in play-acting one may be confronted with different sides of oneself; therein lies the attraction, but also aspects which may frighten. Taking advantage of the slight uncertainty arising in a situation where people in the audience are asked to take part in what is happening implies that the immediate present becomes obviated; we see the area and the factory with open eyes. The real time aspect of VR, providing means of interaction and immersion, then justifies the use of the technology as it interplays well with the nucleus of the account. Comments: The manifestation of an area in Norrköping where there once stood a dye factory constitutes the nucleus of this account. The purpose of the exercise is to determine the extent to which the language of Virtual Reality (VR) communication may be developed
